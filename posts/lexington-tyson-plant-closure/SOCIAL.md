```
Last week, we released our Rentals Within Reach data visualization, which compared the median renter income to the rental stock for all U.S. counties. We said we would come back to it with stories that we believe must be told.

So today, we tell the story of Dawson County, Nebraska, home of Lexington.

Dawson County had the second-best rental affordability of all counties in the country with more than 1,000 rental units and reliable income data. I was hoping to find policies or incentives for low-income housing. I didn't find that. But I did find higher incomes: the median Dawson County renter household made $59,031, considerably higher than the $40,678 in Lincoln County due west. Maybe this was a story of a strong local employer who built roots in this community.

Dawson County, Nebraska was that story, and Tyson was that employer… until January 20, 2026, when Tyson shut the doors on its meatpacking facility, cutting about 3,200 jobs and sending the county into an economic crisis.

In other words, the good news story I was expecting from the data ending in 2024 turned into a nightmare in 2026.

This is this county's story. But it's more than that. It's a story of America, of immigration, and of how one decision can alter the fate of an entire community.

The full report is linked in the first comment.
```

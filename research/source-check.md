# Source check for first-pull.md

Checked October 13, 2026. Each fact in sections 1, 3, 4 and "What has happened
since" of `first-pull.md` that was not pulled directly from Census or BLS was
checked against the page it came from, or a better primary source.

Status key:

- CONFIRMED: the page says it. A short exact quote is given.
- CORRECTED: the page says something different. What it says is given.
- UNCONFIRMED: could not be checked. What was tried is given.

Not checked here, by instruction: Census and BLS figures in section 2, the
LAUS and QCEW figures, and anything labeled "our calculation."

How pages were read: Tyson filings were downloaded from SEC EDGAR and read in
full. News pages were downloaded and read as text. Three pages could only be
read through the fetch tool (Center for American Progress, Kansas City Fed)
or not at all (Nebraska Examiner original, Drovers, Feedstuffs); those are
marked. Anything marked "my arithmetic" is a calculation made for this check,
not a published figure.

### Which number is which

- **$291 million (FY2024) and $426 million (FY2025).** Adjusted operating
  loss, old basis. Confirmed.
- **$1.135 billion (FY2025).** GAAP operating loss, old basis. Confirmed. It
  includes a goodwill impairment, legal accruals, and also $48 million of
  restructuring charges that the notes do not mention.
- **$400 to $600 million.** The November 10, 2025 guidance, old basis.
  Confirmed.
- **$250 to $500 million.** The February 2, 2026 guidance. It is on the new
  basis, so it is not directly comparable to the November range. On the new
  basis the fiscal 2025 adjusted beef loss is $275 million, not $426 million.
  The difference is $151 million (my arithmetic). If the same $151 million
  shift applied to the November range, it would be about $249 to $449 million
  on the new basis (my arithmetic, and an assumption Tyson did not publish).
  Read that way, the February range was close to the November range and not a
  real improvement.
- The guidance then got worse three times: May, August, and September 2026.
- Do not mix the two bases in one chart or one sentence. A fiscal 2019 to
  2025 series exists only on the old basis. Fiscal 2026 quarters exist only
  on the new basis. Fiscal 2023 to 2025 exist on both.

## Tyson's November 21, 2025 announcement (priority 2)

Source: Exhibit 99.1 to Tyson's Form 8-K dated November 21, 2025.
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/100493/000010049325000105/pressreleasedated112125.htm

The release is four short paragraphs. The full body, verbatim:

> Tyson Foods today announced network changes designed to right size its beef
> business and position it for long-term success.
>
> The company will end operations at its Lexington, Nebraska, beef facility
> and convert its Amarillo, Texas, beef facility to a single, full-capacity
> shift. To meet customer demand, production will be increased at other
> company beef facilities, optimizing volumes across our network.
>
> Tyson Foods recognizes the impact these decisions have on team members and
> the communities where we operate. The company is committed to supporting
> our team members through this transition, including helping them apply for
> open positions at other facilities and providing relocation benefits.
>
> With these changes, Tyson Foods is ensuring that it will continue to deliver
> high-quality, affordable, and nutritious protein for generations to come.

What the release does and does not say:

- The only reason given is to "right size its beef business and position it
  for long-term success."
- It does not mention cattle supply, the cattle herd, losses, or the age of
  the plant.
- It gives no closing date and no number of workers.
- It gives no reason for choosing Lexington.

Did Tyson ever give a reason for choosing Lexington? None was found in any
Tyson document read: the November 21 release and 8-K, the FY2025 10-K, the
fiscal 2026 Q1 to Q3 releases, the Q1 and Q3 10-Qs, the August 13 and
September 3, 2026 releases, or the February 2, 2026 earnings call transcript
(Motley Fool copy). Nebraska Public Media wrote on January 23, 2026 that
"Tyson Foods officials haven't discussed the reasons as to why the Lexington
plant was chosen to close." The only account of a plant-specific reason is
second hand, from Gov. Pillen (see section 4 below).

## What plant workers were paid (priority 8)

No hourly wage for the Lexington plant in 2025 was found from Tyson or from a
union. The plant was not unionized. UFCW's statement calls it "a non-union
Tyson Foods beef plant" and says "these workers were not members of UFCW."

What each of the three figures in the notes measures:

| Figure | What it measures | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $94,169 per worker (UNL) | A modeled estimate of total compensation, not Tyson payroll. UNL matched the job titles in the WARN notice to BLS occupational wage data and then added benefits: "Benefits are assumed to equal one-half of wages." That implies average wages of about $62,779 (my arithmetic: 94,169 divided by 1.5). | https://cap.unl.edu/news/economic-impacts-tyson-beef-plant-closure-lexington-nebraska/ |
| $241 million a year (AP), about $75,000 per worker | AP: "Tyson employees alone will lose an estimated $241 million in pay and benefits annually," attributed to the UNL report. The UNL article as published does not contain $241 million. UNL's own inputs give 3,212 times $94,169, or about $302 million (my arithmetic). $241 million divided by 3,212 is about $75,031 (my arithmetic). Where AP's figure came from could not be determined.
